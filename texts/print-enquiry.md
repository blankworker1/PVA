# SUNDIAL · La Meridiana – For the print shop

## The project

**SUNDIAL · La Meridiana** is the first exhibition of PVA (Photovoltaic Art), in Bosa, for seven days around the summer solstice of 2027. Nine old solar panels, hung on walls around the town, power only themselves: no battery, no cables, no internet. Each panel is also a working sundial.

Every day each panel records the sun on its glass, how warm it gets and who stops to visit, and by fixed rules draws an image of it: the dial of its day, sealed at sunset. During the exhibition it can only be seen on a phone, in front of the panel. **Same sun, different data.**

When the exhibition is over, the 63 dials (nine panels by seven days) become a single four-ink screen print, made in Bosa: the whole exhibition on one sheet. That is the print we would like to make and sell with you.

## The print

| Item | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Layout | Square, 9 × 9 grid: 63 image tiles (9 panels × 7 days); "SUNDIAL · La Meridiana" centred in the top row, the dates centred in the bottom row |
| Colours | 4 spot inks, printed light to dark: ochre, red, blue, black |
| Overprint | Translucent inks: red over ochre or blue makes a third colour |
| Artwork | Flat vector shapes, no gradients or halftones |
| Finest lines | To be set with you in millimetres once the print area is known (question 1). Some tiles, the days a panel did not wake, are only a thin black outline |
| Files | We supply the four separations at print size, with registration marks, ready for film |
| Size | As large as your biggest screen allows, image square, paper square |

## What we propose

The shop makes the screens, prints, and sells the prints, which can be ordered at any time after the exhibition. PVA keeps ownership of the screens (or the films, if that is more practical). Every print should match the first, whenever it is made.

## Questions

1. **Print area:** what is the largest image you can print on your biggest screen? This sets the size of every tile and the thinnest line.
2. **Fine lines:** what is the thinnest line you can hold reliably on paper, over four passes?
3. **Paper:** which paper would you suggest for four colours, and can you cut it square (for example 70 × 70 cm from 70 × 100)?
4. **Inks:** do you print water-based inks on paper, and can you add transparent base for the overprint? Can you record the mixes so later prints match?
5. **Registration:** can you register four colours accurately, by press, table or pins?
6. **Proof:** could you pull a small test of red over ochre, and a thin black line, on the chosen paper?
7. **Screens or films:** is it better for PVA to keep four screens, or the films and have screens re-made when needed? Where would they be stored?
8. **Edition:** open (printed to order) or limited and numbered? What would you recommend?
9. **Costs and sales:** set-up costs (films, screens), price per print, and how sale proceeds would be shared.
10. **Timing:** how long from receiving files to the first prints?
11. **Selling:** in the shop, online, or both?

## The sample sheet

Four dials with invented data: two places, the solstice and a day in October. Below, a day the panel did not wake, in black only: the thinnest lines on the print. See `pva_sample_a4.pdf`.

**How to read a dial.** The ring runs from sunrise (left) to sunset (right), with solar noon at the top; it is divided into quarter hours, ochre when the panel is in the sun, blue when it is not. The red line is the panel's heat, rising from sunrise. The black dots are visitors, one for each solar hour. The black bar at the bottom is the horizon and the sunset seal.
