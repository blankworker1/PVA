# Slow Art: Sun Record — 14 Framed Sunshine Recorder Cards

**Production plan · v0.2 · 18 October 2026**
A slow-art production in Bosa, Sardinia: one year of sunshine burnt by the sun itself into 365 daily cards, framed as two septychs of 7 frames (14 frames in all) plus a single solstice card.

The sunshine recorder work is a complementary slow-art production, separate from the SUNDIAL exhibition itself.

**Why the Campbell–Stokes recorder for the main work:** it is the standard instrument, so the work is both art and a genuine meteorological record; one dated card per day; the charred card is a permanent original needing no scanning or fixing; and there are no paper sensitivities or home-made geometry to get right. The main risk moves from the materials to the daily routine (see §8). The silver-paper and cyanotype methods are kept for workshops, where their unpredictability is part of the experience.

## 2. Concept

A Campbell–Stokes sunshine recorder focuses the sun through a glass sphere onto a card behind it. Wherever the sun shines, it burns a trace along the card's hour marks; where cloud passes, the trace breaks. One card is used per day.

Over a year, 365 cards record every hour of sunshine at one spot in Bosa. Framed in order, the series shows the day's sunshine lengthening from December to June and shortening again towards December, with the weather written into every gap.

The work records **sunshine, not daylight**: weak low sun at the ends of the day does not burn, and a fully overcast day leaves a blank card. Both are part of the record.

## 3. Structure: 365 = 7 × 26 + 1 + 7 × 26

## 4. The instrument

**Genuine Campbell–Stokes recorder**, chosen over a home-built burning recorder because it is a proven, unattended outdoor instrument and its official cards are made to char rather than burn.

- Sphere: 101.6 mm optical glass; focal length 74.9 mm (Casella specification)
- Mounted on an equatorial base with latitude adjustment

**Sourcing options**

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| New, Met Office pattern | Made to order; one quote of £1,521.70 with a six-month wait (check current price and lead time). Makers include Fairmount Weather Systems (UK); suppliers include ELE International and Casella |
| Antique original | Occasionally at auction; one late-1800s Negretti & Zambra with cards estimated at £500–£800 |
| Reproduction (India) | About $265–$370 with a year of strips; sphere and card quality unknown — test before relying on it |

**Check before ordering**

- [ ] **Latitude range.** Recorders are made in latitude bands; some are listed for 0–40°. Bosa is about **40.3° N**, on the boundary. Confirm with the supplier which model suits Bosa.
- [ ] **Lead time.** If delivery is after mid-December, start the year at the summer solstice 2027 and use the spring for a test run.

## 5. Cards

- **Three types**, changed by season on the supplier's dates: winter (short curved), equinox (straight), summer (long curved). Some frames will contain two card shapes — part of the record.
- Official cards: special board with a matt turquoise finish; visible trace even in weak sun; treated to char rather than burn.
- **Quantity:** 365 + spares. A typical annual pack is about 380 cards across the three types.
- **Always buy cards from a meteorological supplier**, whichever instrument is used.

**Estimated card size — measure real cards before ordering frames**

| Card | Height (estimated) | Length (estimated) |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | ≈ 38 mm | ≈ 20 cm |
| Equinox | ≈ 42 mm | ≈ 25 cm |
| Summer | ≈ 33 mm | ≈ 28–30 cm |

## 6. Frames

**14 main frames**, all identical, plus one small keystone frame.

| Item | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Main frame | **40 × 115 cm** (working size) | 26 cards stacked, 2–3 mm gaps |
| Keystone frame | ≈ 40 × 15 cm | One card |

**Height check:** 26 equinox cards at ≈ 42 mm plus gaps need ≈ 117 cm, so 115 cm is tight. Options:
- confirm real card heights, then fix the size; or
- increase all frames to **≈ 125 cm**; or
- keep 115 cm and overlap card edges slightly, never covering the traces.

Keep all 14 frames the same size, set by the tallest card type.

**Mounting**
- Align every card on its **noon line** along one central vertical axis: the cards then lengthen and shorten symmetrically through the year.
- Acid-free mounts; corners or hinges, no glue (charred card is fragile).
- **UV-filtering glazing** — the card dye fades in daylight.
- Date written in pencil on the back of each card.

## 7. Hanging

| Layout | Size |
|---|---|
| One row of 14 + keystone | ≈ 7.3 m wide × 1.15–1.25 m tall |
| Two rows of 7, keystone between | ≈ 3.4 m wide × ≈ 2.6 m tall |

In the two-row layout, run the second row **right to left** (June → December). Each frame then sits under the one with the same day lengths (e.g. March above October): the same sun, under different weather.

Display indoors, out of direct sun.

## 8. Daily routine

- **Change the card every day after sunset**, including cloudy days. Write the date on the back.
- Store used cards flat, dry and dark, in date order.
- Keep the sphere clean (dust, salt spray, bird droppings weaken the burn).
- Check level, latitude setting and south alignment weekly; pin the card in place (the bowl has a pin to stop birds moving cards).
- Switch card types on the supplier's seasonal dates.
- A missed day: decide in advance — leave a blank card marked "not recorded", so the series stays at 365.
- **This routine is the project's main risk.** Arrange a named stand-in (or a rota) for every day you will be away, before the year starts.

## 9. Site and safety

- Mount on masonry (stone or brick), on a metal post or bracket, in full sun from the south.
- Nothing combustible nearby: no wooden shutters, awnings, cables, plants or dry vegetation.
- Use official cards only; never paper or wood in the instrument unattended.
- Store and transport the sphere covered — a loose glass sphere can start fires.
- Check Sardinia's regional summer fire-prevention rules; get the site owner's written agreement.

## 10. Workshops (companion activities)

Hands-on sessions where participants make their own sun drawings. These use light-sensitive paper, which is unpredictable over long exposures and so unsuitable for the main record, but ideal for teaching and taking part. Each workshop can open with the recorder itself: the day's card, the sphere, the 365-day project.

### A. One-day cyanotype sun trace

| | |
|---|---|
| Duration | One sunny day (set up in the morning, wash at sunset) |
| Camera | D-box recorders (Appendix A) with a **≈ 1 mm pinhole**, or simple film-canister / tin-can recorders |
| Paper | Cyanotype, bought or hand-coated by participants on watercolour paper |
| Result | A deep blue trace of that day's sun, with gaps for cloud |
| Finish | Wash in water with a few drops of lemon juice; the print is permanent and taken home |

- Can be done in front of an audience: the sun draws during the day, and the image appears in the wash at sunset.
- Coating the paper can be part of the session (prepare sheets the day before; they must dry in the dark).
- No fire risk: pinholes do not concentrate the sun.

### B. Solargraph can, take-home (six months or more)

| | |
|---|---|
| Duration | One session to make; exposure at home for weeks to six months |
| Camera | Drink can sealed on the manual can seamer, pinhole in the side under an opaque peel-off label |
| Paper | Black-and-white RC glossy photographic paper (A4 cut into six 99 × 105 mm pieces for 330 ml cans) |
| Result | The sun's arcs over the landscape, revealed by scanning or photographing the paper and inverting |

- Participants take the can home and mount it facing the equator (south in Europe), tilted back slightly.
- Include printed instructions: mounting, ending the exposure (spare cover sticker), opening in dim light, photographing at once, inverting.
- Optional return service: post the can back to be opened and scanned, building an archive of everyone's sun.
- Loading is done beforehand under red safelight; participants handle only sealed cans.

### C. One-week cyanotype series (optional, longer course)

- D-box recorders with cyanotype, one strip per day for seven days: a small personal septych.
- Shows weather from day to day, and introduces the idea behind the 14-frame work.

### Practical points

- Run test exposures before each workshop: cyanotype speed varies with paper, coating and season.
- Schedule for the sunniest season; have a fallback (cyanotype contact prints of plants or objects) for a cloudy day.
- Materials per participant: one camera, paper, instructions; cyanotype chemicals or pre-coated sheets; wash trays.

## Appendix A — D-box recorder (for workshops)

A home-built polar pinhole recorder. Not used for the main work; used as the camera for workshops A and C.

- D-shaped box: half-cylinder of paper behind a flat front plate; pinhole on the cylinder's axis; axis aligned to the celestial pole (tilted 40.3° up towards north).
- Paper: 105 × 297 mm (A4 cut lengthways), 297 mm round the curve. Inside radius **94.5 mm** (≈ 190 mm diameter; 200 mm PVC drainpipe is close). One 110 mm pipe section, halved, makes two boxes.
- Records 06:00–18:00 solar time; each day a straight line; ≈ 24.7 mm per hour; solstice lines ± 41 mm from centre.
- Pinhole: 0.4 mm (silver paper) or ≈ 1 mm (cyanotype), in **0.05 mm brass shim** on the inside face of a **1–1.5 mm** front plate with a ≈ 30 × 10–15 mm east–west slot.
- Cyanotype version: seven 26-day exposures per box, a septych per site.
